This Coastal Planet

A time-travel playbook for
inspiring coastal communities
By Ruth Catlow and Ann Light

This Coastal Planet works a little differently than most community visioning workshops. It is also not a typical role-playing game. As a facilitator, your role is to guide your group of 15–20 people on a 3-hour journey of imagination, conversation, and shared storytelling — helping them explore the past, present, and future of coastal/marine settlements they care about.

Participants will step into the shoes of children and adults with a burning passion, moving through time and collaboratively creating answers to challenges ahead.

The game is experiential, reflective, and collaborative. Connect across generations. Connect across distance. Connect with coastal and marine life.

Introducing The Four Workshop Stages

The following script is designed to guide participants through a structured, staged journey. Pay close attention to the suggested time allocations to maintain balance, purpose and focus throughout the workshop.

Approx 45 minutes

Stage 1. Welcome, Introductions, The Interspecies Meditation and Sharing Circle

The welcome sets a friendly tone and moves into a practice to sensitise participants to the lives of other species. Each player connects with a partner species.

Approx 20 minutes

Stage 2. Preparation for Play

Participants are asked to add events from personal, global and partner species history to a 200-year timeline running back to 1875 and forward to 2075.

55 minutes

Stage 3. The Play

Participants move into role and participate in 3 scenes. With each scene-change characters age a generation or reincarnate, advocating for their beloved coastal settlement.

20 minutes

Stage 4. De-role and Reflection

De-roling gives participants a chance to step out of their characters and reflect on what they learned about their hopes for themselves, their community, and future generations.

Stage 1. Welcome

5 minutes

Participants arrive and are welcomed at the door. Facilitator A stands near the entrance. Facilitator B arranges pebbles on the central table. Name labels are handed out at the welcome desk.

Facilitator A

Speaking to the room as people settle.

Welcome everyone. We're so glad you're here. Today we're going to travel through time together — not as ourselves exactly, but as characters who care deeply about this coastline and everything that lives here.

"Before we begin, I want to name a few things about where we are and why we're gathered. [Name your location.] This place has a long relationship with the sea — with the creatures, the weather, the industries and communities that have shaped it over generations…"

Note

The humour here is intentional. It eases tension at the start of the role-playing and models a dynamic of care between the two facilitators — making the play feel interesting rather than merely instrumental.
